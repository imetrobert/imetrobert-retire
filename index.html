📈 Inflation — keep your purchasing power

Enter your spending above in today's dollars. Each year it is raised by inflation so you can keep buying the same things. Tax brackets and credits, QPP and OAS are indexed too. No agency publishes a reliable 50-year forecast; the best official ones are the CPP/QPP actuaries' 75-year assumption and the Bank of Canada target, both 2.0%.
RREGOP pensions are only partly indexed to inflation (about half for service since 2000 — check your statement).

Locked-in account (LIRA / LIF)

Bell Canada is federally regulated, so a Bell DC pension typically locks into a federal LIRA/LIF on leaving the plan, not a Quebec-jurisdiction one. Jurisdiction affects unlocking rules and maximum withdrawal %.

Bell pension (DC)

Kept apart from your LIRA so the two never overwrite each other — fill it in yourself, or import it from your ETF tracker's Pension tab. The model treats it as its own federal locked-in pool (OSFI maximum withdrawal, and it is the money eligible for the RLIF unlock below) while the LIRA above follows the jurisdiction you chose — so a Quebec LIRA plus a federal Bell pension are each handled under their own rules. Federal LIFs have a legal maximum set by OSFI each year. Leave this blank and the tool uses the published OSFI formula for your age (about 5.2% at 55, 6.0% at 65, 6.9% at 70 in 2026); enter your own % only to override it. the CRA minimum withdrawal % is always enforced regardless. Applies to federal LIFs only — Quebec LIFs have no maximum from age 55, so this is ignored if Quebec is selected.
Federally-regulated LIRA/LIF holders get a one-time right to transfer up to 50% of the account's value into a Restricted Life Income Fund (RLIF) and then out into a regular unlocked RRIF/RRSP — permanently freeing that portion from the federal LIF maximum withdrawal limit (it still follows the standard CRA RRIF minimum once you start drawing on it, same as the rest of your RRSP). The transfer itself is not taxable when it happens and does not use up any personal RRSP contribution room.
Federal rule: the one-time 50% unlock is only available from the year you turn 55 (by moving to a restricted LIF). Ages below 55 are raised to 55.
Must be at or after your retirement age above (this tool only simulates year-by-year from retirement onward — if you enter an age before that, the transfer is applied at your first retirement year instead). One trade-off worth knowing: LIF/RLIF income always qualifies for the $2,000 pension income tax credit regardless of age, but once unlocked into a regular RRIF it only qualifies once you turn 65 — the schedule below reflects this.

Growth

How fast your money grows, and how long it needs to last.

Returns and inflation. Enter the returns you expect below either as nominal (the headline figure, e.g. 6.5%) or as real (after inflation, e.g. 4.5%) — choose which on Step 2 under “Inflation”. If you pick “None — today’s dollars”, enter real returns, because spending and tax brackets then stay flat. Mixing the two makes a plan look more or less optimistic than it is.
Payout frequency (set on Step 2) affects growth too. Withdrawing weekly instead of monthly leaves money invested slightly longer on average between withdrawals, so it earns a bit more growth over the year — the post-retirement return below is applied accounting for whichever frequency you chose. The difference is usually small (a few tenths of a percent of the balance per year) but it's modeled exactly rather than assumed away.

OAS Clawback (Pension Recovery Tax)

In couple mode, the clawback is calculated by splitting total household taxable income evenly between spouses (a simplification — real clawback is based on each person's own individual income, which this tool doesn't track separately income-source by income-source).

Quebec Senior Credits (age 65+)

These three credits are added together, reduced by this rate on family income above the threshold, then multiplied by the Quebec credit rate above.

RRIF / LIF Minimum Withdrawal Rates (CRA)

% of your January 1 balance that must come out each year. Under age 71 the legislated formula 1 ÷ (90 − age) is used automatically (e.g. 2.86% at 55). From 71 these table values apply. The same minimums apply to RRIFs and LIFs, federal or Quebec.

Federal LIF Maximum — OSFI Reference Rate

OSFI resets this each January (2026: 0.0349). It drives the federal LIF maximum withdrawal used when you leave the "LIF Maximum %" field blank. Check: age 65 should give 6.03% for 2026.

Lump-Sum Withholding on Registered Withdrawals (Quebec)

What your institution deducts at the moment of a withdrawal above the RRIF/LIF minimum. Combined rate = federal rate for the tier (the reduced rate for Quebec residents) + the flat Quebec rate, applied to the whole payment, per account. It's a prepayment of your tax, not extra tax.

Inflation Assumptions

Used when you pick an inflation option on the main form. Long-run rate = Bank of Canada target (also the CPP/QPP actuaries' ultimate assumption). Near-term path = the government actuaries' CPI projections for the next year(s), then the long-run rate.

🎉 Life events

One-time lump sums, and costs that repeat each year. Tap a card to fill it in.

Optional. Use this for a one-time expense that isn't part of your regular ongoing spending — a wedding, a health crisis, a major home repair, helping a family member, etc. Enter up to 10 events: a name, the lump sum you'll need, and the age and month you'll need it.
How the model funds each event: in the order that costs the least tax — TFSA first (tax-free, no income or OAS clawback impact), then non-registered savings (only the gain portion is taxed), and only then your registered accounts (RRSP/LIRA/FTQ) as a last resort, since those are fully taxable and could push you into a higher bracket or trigger OAS clawback that year. The results screen shows exactly how each event ended up being funded, and the schedule table flags which year(s) had one.
The month is used for your own planning reference — this tool simulates year-by-year, so it doesn't change the math within a year, only which age/year the lump sum lands in.

🔁 Recurring events — repeat every year

Complements the one-time events above — use this for a cost that repeats every year over a stretch of your retirement, like a seasonal condo, a recurring family commitment, or an ongoing care cost. Example: a Florida condo costing $6,000/year, every December, from age 65 through 85 — set Amount = 6,000, Month = December, Start Age = 65, End Age = 85. It's funded the exact same tax-efficient way each year it's active (TFSA → non-registered → registered, as described above) and combines with any one-time event happening in the same year.
